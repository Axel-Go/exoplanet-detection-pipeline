What it does

  1. Download

    Pulls a TESS light curve for each target from the MAST archive — around 18,000 brightness measurements over 27 days.

  2. Search

    Runs a box-least-squares periodogram across 20,000 trial periods from 0.5 to 10 days, hunting for a dip that repeats.

  3. Decide

    Accepts the strongest peak only if it clears a detection threshold and the implied planet isn't too big to be a planet.

  4. Grade

    Checks every verdict against the archive: a match, a harmonic, a wrong period, a miss, or a false alarm.

Plenty of tutorials find one planet in a light curve already known to contain one. This does the harder version — {{ stats.n_known }} stars with a confirmed transiting planet and {{ stats.n_control }} nearby stars with none, searched identically, with the answers withheld until scoring.

Both numbers matter. A detector that flags everything has perfect recall and is useless; one that flags nothing never raises a false alarm and is equally useless. The interesting part is where the trade sits, and what the failures have in common.
